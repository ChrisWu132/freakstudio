Boards, parts and the BOM behind them.

Schematic and PCB work, BOM cleanup, sourcing with approved alternatives, and assembled boards and harnesses.

Who this is for

A development-board or breadboard prototype that needs its own board, a PCB that needs revising, or a BOM that has to be bought and built, including older and hard-to-find chips.

Typical starting points

  • A breadboard or development-board prototype
  • An existing schematic or PCB to review or revise
  • A BOM with parts that are hard to find, slow to arrive or no longer made

What Anvol does

  • Schematic and PCB review
  • Schematic, PCB layout and power design
  • BOM cleanup
  • Component sourcing, including older and hard-to-find chips
  • Alternative parts identified and checked; none used without your approval
  • Component lifecycle check
  • PCBA and wiring harnesses
  • Production files
  • Parts purchasing for small batches

Deliverables

  • Schematic, PCB files and Gerbers
  • A full BOM with a source for each part, costed from real parts and supplier quotes and checked by an engineer
  • Approved alternative parts
  • Assembled boards and harnesses

Timeline and cost drivers

Timeline
Set mostly by part availability. The BOM review shows which parts are slow to get, and the dates go in the written scope.
Cost depends on
Board complexity, number of revisions and part availability. Each board-assembly run pays a fixed machine setup charge, so the quantity matters too.
Small batches
In batches of 5 to 20, parts are about 40% of unit cost and PCB and assembly about 40%. Costs →

What is excluded

  • Certification testing (FCC/CE). It is done by a test lab and quoted separately when a product needs it.
  • Using an alternative part without your approval.
  • Shipping and import duties. Usually arranged by you once units leave the factory.
  • Anything outside the written scope. Major scope changes are re-quoted, with a new timeline, before work continues.
