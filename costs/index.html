What a hardware prototype actually costs.

Real development fees and per-unit prices from devices our engineers built and delivered. Not ranges, not estimates: what each project cost.

Four kinds of cost

Development fee
Engineering, design, integration and project work.
Prototype cost
Building and testing the first units.
Pilot batch cost
Producing a small validated batch.
Repeat batch cost
Repeating production once the design and process are stable.

Three delivered projects

ProjectUnitsPer unitDevelopmentTotal
24-channel logic analyzer15$20$800$1,100
Lidar line-following car10$150$5,000$6,500
Swing-arm tracked robot4$5,000$10,000$30,000

Total = development fee + units × per-unit price. Most of the difference between projects is the development fee, which follows the engineering work, not the unit count.

Why small batches cost more per unit

Parts
About 40% of unit cost in batches of 5 to 20.
PCB and assembly
About 40%. Each board-assembly run pays a fixed machine setup charge.
Enclosure
About 15%.
Final assembly and test
About 5%.

Development fee, as a rule of thumb

Simple small device
About $500 to $3,000.
Larger robot
About $10,000 to $20,000.
Note
These are our engineers' experience ranges, not quotes. Compare against the three projects above.

What totals leave out

Certification
None of the three went through FCC or CE. Quoted separately when a product needs it.
Shipping and duties
Usually arranged by the customer once units leave the factory.
Extras
Tutorials, training or a live demo.
100 or 1,000 units
Not published. None of these were made at that volume, and small-batch prices should not be stretched to it.
